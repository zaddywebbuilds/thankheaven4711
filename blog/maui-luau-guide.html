Best Luaus in Maui: A Visitor's Guide

A luau at its best is a legitimate and meaningful celebration rooted in Hawaiian tradition -- storytelling through hula, the uncovering of an imu-roasted pig, and a communal feast. At its worst it is a buffet in a parking lot with a fire knife dancer. Knowing the difference before you book saves $120 per person and an evening.

What a Good Luau Actually Is

The word luau originally referred to a specific dish (cooked taro leaves), not the feast itself, which is more accurately called an 'aha'aina. Modern luaus are evening events that typically include: the imu ceremony (unveiling the kalua pig cooked in an underground oven), a buffet of Hawaiian and Pacific food, open bar, and a performance of hula and traditional Pacific Island dance. The best ones prioritize storytelling and cultural context. The worst ones are designed to move tables quickly and maximize upsell revenue.

A good luau runs $100 to $200 per person. If you see pricing significantly below that, look carefully at what is included and what is not.

Old Lahaina Luau

Widely considered the best luau in Maui and among the best in Hawaii. The cultural programming is serious -- the hula performed here follows traditional forms and the historical storytelling is genuinely informative. The food quality is a step above most competitors. It runs on the beach at sunset on the west side and the setting adds to the experience. Currently operating in a new location following the Lahaina fire. Check current details and reserve well in advance -- it sells out months ahead in peak season. Around $150 to $180 per person.

Feast at Lele

A more intimate, higher-end alternative from the same operators as Old Lahaina. Feast at Lele is a sit-down dinner (not a buffet) where each course represents a different Pacific culture -- Hawaii, Tahiti, Samoa, New Zealand -- paired with traditional performance from that culture. The experience is more curated and more expensive ($150 to $200+ per person) but also less crowded and more personal. An excellent option for a honeymoon or special occasion.

Myths of Maui (Royal Lahaina Resort)

A larger-scale production luau on the beach at Ka'anapali. Consistently solid quality for the scale -- the grounds are beautiful, the production is professional, and the buffet is expansive. Less intimate than Old Lahaina but good for groups or families who want the full commercial luau experience with reliable execution. Around $120 to $160 per person.

Kihei to the luau in 45 minutes

Unit 711 in Kihei is about 45 minutes from the west side luau venues. Easy evening drive, and you come home to a private west-facing lanai and open ocean. A good trade for the resort luau crowd.

Te Au Moana (Wailea)

The most convenient luau for visitors staying in South Maui. Te Au Moana runs at the Wailea Beach Marriott and the beachfront setting is genuinely beautiful. The quality is above average for a hotel-based luau. Not as culturally rich as Old Lahaina, but a solid option if you do not want to drive to the west side. Around $120 to $160 per person. Book directly with the resort.

What to Skip

Generic hotel luaus at large resort properties outside of the above recommendations tend to offer lower production values and food quality. The Ka'anapali Beach Hotel luau and some of the South Maui hotel offerings fall into this category. The price may be lower, but the experience is often noticeably less. If you are going to spend $100+ per person, the quality difference between Old Lahaina and a generic hotel luau is significant enough to justify the drive.

Free Alternatives

If the cost of a formal luau does not fit the budget, several free or low-cost alternatives offer genuine Hawaiian cultural experience. The Shops at Wailea and various outdoor venues in Kihei host free hula performances and Hawaiian music events. Check local event boards for current listings. The Pacific Whale Foundation also runs free educational events during whale season. See our guide to Hawaiian cultural experiences for more options.
