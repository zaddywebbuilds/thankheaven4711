What Snorkeling Gear to Bring to Maui

Maui snorkeling is excellent enough to do every day of your trip. Whether you rent everything, bring your own mask, or buy gear at Costco in Kahului, the right answer depends on how often you snorkel, your face shape, and how much space you have in your bag.

The Mask: Bring Your Own

The mask is the one item most worth bringing from home. Rental masks are cleaned between uses, but they are sized to fit a wide range of face shapes and typically seal adequately for most people -- not perfectly for everyone. If you have ever had a rental mask leak around the nose or fog constantly, that is a fit issue, not a water quality issue.

A mask that fits your face well makes the difference between comfortable snorkeling and constantly adjusting equipment. If you snorkel even once a year, a $30 to $60 mask is a worthwhile investment. Mid-range models from Cressi, U.S. Divers, or Mares outperform most rental gear and pack flat in a checked bag.

On full-face masks: These are popular but have real drawbacks for Maui snorkeling. They are bulkier to pack, and more critically, they can retain CO2 during extended surface sessions, which causes headaches and disorientation. A traditional two-piece mask and snorkel is safer and more comfortable for most people.

The Snorkel: Bring or Rent, Does Not Matter Much

A snorkel is cheap, light, and the rental ones are consistently cleaned. If you already own one, bring it. If you do not, a rental snorkel is perfectly adequate. Dry-top snorkels (with a valve that blocks water if you submerge the tip) are convenient for beginners but add slight breathing resistance. Purge valves that let you clear water with a sharp exhale are useful. Both features are available in rental stock on Maui.

Fins: Rent Them

Fins are the most annoying gear to travel with -- bulky, heavy, and awkward in a bag. Maui has rental snorkel shops at every major beach area and many accommodation complexes. Rentals typically run $5 to $12 per day or $15 to $30 per week. Quality is adequate for reef snorkeling. Unless you are a serious diver with preferred fin specifications, leave them at home.

Unit 711 is directly above the reef. Grab your mask, walk down the lawn, and you are in. No drive, no parking, no fee. The best snorkeling spots are all within minutes.

Wetsuit or Rash Guard?

The water off Maui runs 74 to 80 degrees Fahrenheit year-round. Most visitors are comfortable in a rash guard and trunks. A full wetsuit is unnecessary for typical reef snorkeling -- it is designed for dive depths where water is colder, and it adds considerable drag on the surface. A 3mm shorty wetsuit can be useful in winter (January to March) for anyone who runs cold. Rental wetsuits are available from dive shops and snorkel tour operators.

A rash guard is more useful than a wetsuit for most visits. It provides UV protection (critical in Maui's equatorial sun), some thermal benefit, and protects against accidental coral contact. Bring one from home. Maui sells them, but selection is limited and prices are high.

Where to Rent Gear in Kihei

Several shops along South Kihei Road rent full snorkel sets (mask, snorkel, fins) for around $10 to $15 per day. Snorkel Bob's is the most widely known and has multiple island locations. Boss Frog's and Maui Dive Shop also rent gear. If you are staying at unit 711, the closest shops are a short drive along South Kihei Road.

Buying Gear in Maui

Costco in Kahului sells basic snorkel sets (mask, snorkel, fins) for $20 to $35 -- reasonable quality for casual use. If you plan to snorkel daily during a week-long trip, buying at Costco and leaving the gear at the unit (or giving it to the next guests) is often cheaper than daily rentals. Walmart in Kahului is another option for basic sets.
