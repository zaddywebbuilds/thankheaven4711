Best Coffee Shops in Kihei, Maui

Kihei coffee shops Maui visitors can count on are not always the ones with the biggest signs. This is a working guide to where the espresso is good, the lines are short enough to matter, and the seating lets you plan the rest of the day without rushing.

Kihei runs roughly four miles along South Kihei Road, from the Maalaea end down past Wailea. That stretch holds a lot of coffee, and the difference between a forgettable cup and a genuinely good one usually comes down to two things: whether they pull real espresso and whether they open early enough to beat your snorkel plans.

Below are the categories worth knowing, whether you are renting a condo at 3559 Lower Honoapiilani Rd #11 and want to walk for your morning flat white or you are driving through on the way to Lahaina.

Coffee for the Early Snorkel Crew

This is the practical reality of Kihei. Trade winds are calmest before about 9 a.m. and the water off the Kamaole beaches is clearest early, so anyone serious about snorkeling is up before the sun clears Haleakala. A few walk-up espresso windows and coffee bars along South Kihei Road open at 6 or 6:30 a.m., which is exactly what you want.

Order the night before if you can. Most condo kitchens come with a drip machine, and that gets you out the door at first light without waiting in a line at all. At unit 711, we keep the kitchen stocked for exactly that reason -- you can brew at 5:45, pour it into a travel mug, and be in the water before the parking lot at Kamaole II fills. Then reward yourself with a proper latte on the way back. The best snorkeling spots near Kihei are all within a five-minute drive.

Local Roasters and Small-Batch Espresso

Maui has a real coffee-growing tradition on the slopes of Haleakala, in Kula and Olinda, and a handful of Kihei cafes carry island-grown beans alongside a rotating guest espresso. Look for single-origin Maui coffee on the shelf, roasted within the last couple of weeks rather than packed with a distant roast date. The better shops will list the roast date on the bag, and if they do not, ask.

Expect to pay between $4 and $5 for a 12-ounce latte and around $18 to $24 for a 12-ounce bag of local beans. A flat white or cortado is the fastest way to judge a shop: if the milk is stretched properly and the shot is not sour, the rest of the menu is usually solid. Many of these cafes double as breakfast counters, so you can get a breakfast burrito or an acai bowl in the same stop. Cafes Built for a Slow Morning

Not every breakfast needs to be a dash. The pleasure of a Kihei morning is sitting outside with a second cup while the trade winds pick up and the palms start moving. Several cafes along the strip have shaded patios with tables spaced far enough apart that you can spread out a map or a laptop and stay a while. These are the ones to choose when you have nowhere to be until lunch.

If you want the view without the table, make coffee at the condo and drink it on the lanai. Our 35-foot west-facing lanai looks out over the AuAu Channel toward Lanai and Molokai, and on a clear morning the water is that impossible turquoise before the wind roughens it. It is a better seat than most patios in town, and the coffee is whatever you like.

Work-Friendly Spots with Wi-Fi

Anyone on a partial work trip will want a reliable connection and an outlet. A few Kihei cafes have solid Wi-Fi and are relaxed about laptops during the slower mid-morning window between 9 and 11 a.m. After that, tables turn over quickly and it is courteous to give yours up. Cell coverage in Kihei is generally good, so a hotspot is a reasonable backup.

Our unit has fast Wi-Fi too, plus a desk and a full kitchen, which means a working morning can start with espresso at home instead of an $8 café order. It is one of the quieter arguments for a condo over a resort. We wrote about that trade-off in why a Maui condo beats a hotel.

Afternoon Coffee, Shave Ice, and the Heat

By 1 p.m. the sun in Kihei is unforgiving and a hot latte loses its appeal. This is when the local habit shifts to iced coffee, cold brew, or a shave ice from one of the stands near the beaches. Cold brew is the safer bet if you want something that tastes like coffee rather than dessert. Several shops keep a nitro tap going, which is a good sign they take it seriously.

Parking along South Kihei Road is free but tight from about 10 a.m. onward. The lots behind the strip malls are easier, and the walk is short either way. Bring a refillable bottle -- most cafes will fill it, and you will want it on the beach.

What to Know Before You Go

A few details that save time. Many small Kihei cafes close by 2 or 3 p.m., so an afternoon craving needs a plan. Some close one day a week, often Monday or Tuesday, and hours shift seasonally, so check the day of. Tipping works as it does on the mainland, and card payment is nearly universal, though a little cash is handy at smaller stands.

If you are staying at 3559 Lower Honoapiilani Rd #11, most of these spots are a five- to ten-minute drive or a pleasant walk along the road, and you are close to Kamaole Beach Parks I, II and III. Pair a coffee run with a beach morning and you have the shape of a very good Kihei day. For more of those, see our list of things to do in Kihei, and when the season turns, the whales start showing up in the channel right off the lanai -- we cover that in the humpback whale watching guide.

Coffee is a small part of a Maui trip, but it is the part that sets the tone for the morning. Get it right and the rest of the day tends to follow.
