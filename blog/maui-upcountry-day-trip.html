Upcountry Maui Day Trip: Kula, Makawao and Beyond

October 15, 2026 Day trips 9 min read

Most visitors spend their whole Maui vacation within earshot of the surf, and that is a fine plan. But an upcountry Maui day trip delivers something the coast cannot: 60-degree mornings, eucalyptus fog, farm stands selling strawberries still warm from the sun, and a view of both the ocean and the volcano at once. It is about 40 minutes from Kihei if you time the traffic right.

Why Upcountry Feels Like a Different Island

Haleakala rises to 10,023 feet, and the road climbing its flank passes through five distinct climate zones. Kihei might be 86 degrees and breezy when you pull out of the parking garage at 4711 S Kihei Rd. By the time you reach Pukalani, you will be reaching for a jacket and wondering where the clouds came from. The temperature drops roughly 3 degrees for every 1,000 feet of elevation, so plan on 15 to 20 degrees cooler than the coast, plus wind.

That climate is exactly why the upcountry exists as an agricultural region. Kula means "open country" in Hawaiian, and the volcanic soil plus cool nights produce things that will not grow in Kihei -- onions, potatoes, lettuce, flowers, wine grapes, and some of the best tomatoes in the state.

The Route: Kihei to Kula and Makawao

The most efficient loop from South Maui runs Maui Veterans Highway to the Hana Highway junction, then up Haleakala Highway through Pukalani to Kula. From there, work back downhill through Makawao and take Baldwin Avenue or Haleakala Highway back to the coast. Total driving is roughly 70 miles, and you can stretch it to a full day easily.

Leave Kihei by 8 a.m. Two reasons: morning cloud cover often sits on the upper slopes by noon, and the Kula farm stands sell out of the good stuff. If you only have half a day, skip the summit and stay between Kula and Makawao.

Kula: Farms, Lavender, and Wine

Alii Kula Lavender sits at 4,000 feet and is the easiest introduction to the region. The gardens are modest but the views toward the West Maui Mountains and the isthmus are excellent, and the scones are legitimately worth the stop.

For produce, drive Kula Highway and watch for roadside stands. Kula Country Farms at the intersection of Kula Highway and Omaopio Road is the most reliable, with a pick-your-own strawberry patch in season and a view that reaches down to the ocean. MauiWine at Ulupalakua Ranch, about 20 minutes further along the same road, offers tastings in a spot that feels like a completely different island -- old ranch land, towering trees, and no traffic.

If you have kids or a picnic planned, the Kula Botanical Garden is a compact 8-acre property that takes about an hour and stays cool even at midday.

Come home to the ocean side of the island

After a cool morning in Kula, the 35-foot west-facing lanai at unit 711 is where the day should end -- warm, breezy, and looking straight out at Lanai and Molokai.

Makawao: Paniolo Town With a Real Personality

Makawao was a ranching town long before it was a shopping destination, and the paniolo (Hawaiian cowboy) history still shows in the storefronts along Makawao Avenue. It is a 15-minute drive downhill from Kula and the perfect lunch stop.

Practical picks: Komoda Store and Bakery for cream puffs, but go before 10 a.m. because they sell out. Makawao Garden Cafe for a shaded courtyard lunch. And the Makawao History Museum, which is small, free-ish, and genuinely interesting if you want the ranching backstory.

Parking is the main friction point. The public lot behind Makawao Avenue is the easy answer -- do not circle Baldwin Avenue hoping for street parking.

Beyond: Kula Forest Reserve and the Summit Road

If you have a full day and a vehicle with decent clearance, two upgrades are worth considering. The first is Kula Forest Reserve, reachable via Waipoli Road, where the cypress and eucalyptus forest turns into a genuinely surreal fog-forest landscape. The paved section is short and the road beyond is rough, so check conditions first.

The second is the Haleakala summit itself. If you are not doing a sunrise trip, a mid-morning drive to the 9,740-foot summit overlook is quieter and warmer. The road from Kula is about 45 minutes with no services, so top off your tank in Pukalani. Our Haleakala sunrise guide covers the full drive and reservation details if you want to go earlier.

Practical Tips for the Drive

Layers. A hoodie and long pants in the trunk will make the whole day better. It is not a myth -- it is genuinely cold up there by coastal standards.

Water and snacks. Services thin out past Pukalani. Bring more than you think you need.

Sun protection anyway. You are closer to the sun at 4,000 feet and the UV is stronger, even when it is cloudy and cool.

Watch for cows and chickens. Seriously. Free-range cattle cross the road in the Ulupalakua area, and the wild chickens in Makawao have zero fear.

Time it right. If you are visiting during whale season, November through May, you will see humpbacks in the AuAu Channel from the higher viewpoints on the way down. Our guide to whale watching from a Maui lanai covers where to look.

Pair it with a beach day. The beauty of basing yourself in Kihei is that upcountry is a morning trip, not an expedition. You can be back on the sand by 2 p.m. If you want more ideas for filling the rest of the week, see our roundup of things to do in Kihei.

Getting Back to the Coast

The descent into Kihei is one of the better drives on Maui. You come down through the last of the eucalyptus, the temperature climbs, the ocean opens up, and by the time you park, you are ready for a swim.

That contrast is the whole point of staying on the South Maui coast. Unit 711 at 4711 S Kihei Rd gives you reef snorkeling off the lawn, turtles on the shoreline, and a west-facing lanai that catches the sunset over Lanai and Molokai. Pair a cool upcountry morning with an oceanfront evening and you have used a day well.
