Best Coffee Maui: Where to Drink on Your South Maui Vacation

Maui grows coffee on the slopes of Haleakala, roasts it within sight of the ocean, and serves it in cafes where the walk from the parking lot takes longer than the pour. Finding the best coffee Maui has to offer means knowing which spots are worth the drive from Kihei and which ones you can reach before you have finished waking up.

Start With the In-Room Setup at Unit 711

Coffee at a vacation rental lives or dies on two things: the machine and the mug you carry to the lanai. Unit 711 has a full kitchen, so you are not stuck with a single-serve pod and a paper cup. Buy a bag of local beans on your first full day, keep it in the freezer, and you have a reason to be up before sunrise without waiting in a line anywhere.

The reward for that early alarm is the lanai. The west-facing deck at our oceanfront condo at 3559 Lower Honoapiilani Rd #11 runs 35 feet along the seventh floor, looking out over the AuAu Channel toward Lanai and Molokai. From November through May, humpback whales surface out there while your coffee is still hot. No cafe on the island has that view. That is not a knock on the cafes. It is just a fact.

The Kihei Standbys Within a Few Minutes

Kihei's coffee scene is built for people in boardshorts and sandy flip-flops. You will find a handful of reliable spots along South Kihei Road and in the shopping centers near Piilani Highway, most of them open by 6:30 or 7:00 a.m. Expect local espresso, cold brew on tap, and pastries that sell out early on weekends.

Two things worth knowing. First, parking in the busier plazas around the Kalama Beach area fills fast after 8:00 a.m., so go early or walk. Second, if you are heading to the beach anyway, grab your coffee before you drive. Standing in a cafe line at 10:00 a.m. on a Saturday is the least efficient way to spend a Maui morning.

If you want the coffee stop to be part of a fuller meal, our guide to the best restaurants in Kihei covers the plate lunch spots and dinner tables that pair well with a lazy South Maui day.

Upcountry Roasters Are Worth the Drive

The coffee grown on Maui comes from the upcountry slopes above Makawao and Kula, and several estates there offer farm tours and tasting rooms. It is a genuine detour: roughly an hour from Kihei up Haleakala Highway, cooler by ten to fifteen degrees, and often wet in the afternoon.

Go in the morning. The drive up is beautiful, the tasting rooms are quieter before noon, and you can loop back through Makawao for lunch. Buy whole beans rather than ground. They travel better, and a bag of Maui-grown coffee makes a far better souvenir than another refrigerator magnet.

Prices at the source are usually a little better than what the same beans cost in a resort-area shop, though not dramatically. The real reason to go is the drive and the view down the isthmus toward the West Maui Mountains. That view is worth the gas.

What to Order and What to Skip

Maui coffee culture leans local and unpretentious. A few practical notes for visitors:

Ask for the local roast. Many shops pour a house blend made with beans from elsewhere in the islands or further afield. If you want to taste Maui specifically, ask which beans were grown here. Staff almost always know, and they usually appreciate the question.

Cold brew is the move in summer. From June through September, the Kihei coast runs hot and humid by mid-morning. A cold brew over ice survives the walk to the beach better than a hot latte does.

Skip the drive-through habit. Maui does not really do the mainland drive-through thing well, and the local shops are not much slower. You are on vacation. Stand outside for four minutes.

Bring a travel mug. Some shops discount refills, and you will use it daily. Our Maui packing list has a short section on the small items people forget, and a reusable mug is one of them.

Building a Coffee Morning Into Your Maui Day

The best version of a coffee morning on Maui goes like this. Wake before the sun, brew a pot in the kitchen at the condo, and carry it out to the lanai while the sky over Lanai goes from gray to orange. Watch the reef off the lawn for a few minutes. Then drive to a Kihei cafe for a second cup and something with butter in it, because you earned it.

From there, the day opens up. Snorkeling off the rocks, a drive south to Makena, or nothing at all. The point of a good coffee stop is not the coffee. It is the hour it gives you before the rest of the day starts.
