Haleakala Sunrise: Complete Visitor Guide

Watching the sun rise from the summit of Haleakala at 10,023 feet -- above the clouds, in near-freezing air, with the entire Hawaiian island chain spread below you -- is one of the most extraordinary experiences available to any visitor to Maui. It requires planning, an early alarm, and the right clothing. Here is everything you need to know.

Book Your Timed-Entry Reservation First

This is the step most visitors discover too late. The National Park Service requires a timed-entry reservation for sunrise visits (3 a.m. to 7 a.m.) at Haleakala. Reservations go on sale 60 days in advance at recreation.gov and sell out within minutes of the window opening. If you want to see sunrise at the summit, book your reservation on the exact date that is 60 days before your planned visit -- set a calendar reminder and check at midnight Hawaii time.

If you missed the 60-day window, a small number of day-of reservations are released at 7 a.m. two days prior. These go fast. Worth checking but not worth counting on.

The Drive Up from Kihei

From Maui Sands Seaside in Kihei, the summit is about 38 miles and 90 minutes in average conditions. But sunrise happens early, and you need to be at the summit before it. Add 45 minutes for the summit approach (the last stretch is slow due to switchbacks and altitude effects on some vehicles). That means:

  • Check your reservation time and subtract 30 minutes for being parked and at the viewpoint.
  • Add 90 minutes for the drive from Kihei.
  • Add 20 minutes buffer for slow traffic or unexpected stops.

For a 5:45 a.m. sunrise, leave the unit by 3:45 a.m. Yes, that means a 3:15 a.m. alarm. It is worth it.

What to Wear

The temperature at 10,023 feet runs 30 to 55 degrees Fahrenheit at sunrise. Wind chill can push the perceived temperature well below freezing. This surprises visitors who arrived in Maui with only swimwear and sun dresses.

  • Thermal or fleece base layer
  • A warm mid-layer (fleece jacket or heavy hoodie)
  • A wind and waterproof outer layer
  • Gloves and a beanie or warm hat
  • Long pants -- not shorts
  • Closed-toe shoes -- not sandals

If you did not pack for cold, stores in Kahului (Walmart, Target) carry affordable layers and beanies. Pick them up the day before.

At the Summit

Park at the Haleakala Visitor Center (elevation 9,740 feet) or the summit observation building at Pu'u Ula'ula Overlook (10,023 feet). The visitor center tends to have more space. Both offer direct sunrise views. Arrive 20 to 30 minutes before sunrise to find your spot -- the viewpoints fill up quickly, especially in high season.

The sunrise sequence takes about 20 minutes. First a brightening on the eastern horizon, then the sky goes gold, then the sun clears the horizon and lights up the cloud layer below you in pink and orange. On a clear day you can see the Big Island, Oahu, and Molokai from the summit. Clouds obscure the view about 30 percent of the time. You cannot predict it -- that is part of the deal.

Base in Kihei, day trip to the summit

Unit 711 at Maui Sands Seaside is 38 miles from the Haleakala summit. Come back from the drive and sleep in. Or watch whales from the lanai for the rest of the morning.

After the Sunrise

The park is worth exploring after sunrise if you have the energy. The Sliding Sands Trail descends into the crater -- red cinder, silence, and an otherworldly landscape unlike anything else in Hawaii. Even a 30-minute walk down (and back up -- back up is harder at altitude) gives a sense of the crater's scale.

Breakfast on the way back down in Kula or Makawao in Upcountry Maui is the natural next stop. The old Kula Lodge has views. Makawao has a good bakery. Either way, most guests are back at the unit by 10 a.m. with a full day still ahead.

Sunrise vs Sunset at Haleakala

Sunset is also stunning and does not require a special reservation. If you cannot book the sunrise window or do not want the 3 a.m. start, a late-afternoon drive up and sunset at the crater is a legitimate alternative. The colors are similarly dramatic. You arrive when you want and leave when the light fades.
