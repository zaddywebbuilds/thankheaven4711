Maui Snorkeling in November: Water Conditions and Best Spots

November sits in a sweet spot on the Maui snorkeling calendar. The summer crowds have thinned, the trade winds tend to soften before winter sets in, and the first humpback whales start appearing in the channel. Offshore visibility on a calm morning can stretch past 80 feet. Here is what the water actually does in November, how to read the daily conditions from a West Maui lanai, and where to put your mask on.

What Water Conditions Look Like in November

Ocean temperature off West Maui in November typically runs between 77 and 79 F. That is warm enough for a comfortable hour in the water in a swimsuit, though many people bring a thin 1mm rash guard for morning sessions or when the breeze picks up on the return swim. You do not need a full wetsuit. You do need sun protection, because the November sun in Hawaii still carries real bite and the reflection off the water doubles the exposure on your back.

Visibility is the headline. On a flat morning with light wind, you can usually see 40 to 80 feet, and occasionally more off Honokowai and Napili. That is noticeably better than the hazy, wind-churned afternoons of late summer. The reason comes down to swell and wind. November often lands between the south swells of summer and the big north swells of deep winter, so the reef stays comparatively calm, and the water column stays clear. When a north swell does arrive, it usually affects the north-facing and northwest-facing shorelines more than the sheltered south and west pockets.

Surf is the variable to watch. A passing northwest swell can raise wave height for a day or two and stir up sediment along the shoreline, dropping visibility to 15 or 20 feet and making entry at rocky spots awkward. That is normal. Give it a day, and it clears. The reef off the West Maui coast recovers fast because the water is deep close to shore and the currents flush sediment quickly.

Trade Winds, Kona Storms, and Reading the Forecast

The trade winds that dominate Maui most of the year typically ease in November. They still blow, but more often as gentle 10 to 15 mph winds rather than the 20-plus of summer. That means more glassy mornings and better afternoon water than you might expect elsewhere on the calendar. The trade wind pattern is your friend: it usually builds through the late morning, peaks around 2 or 3 p.m., and drops off again near sunset.

The thing to watch for in November is a Kona low. These are low-pressure systems that spin up southwest of the islands and pull warm, humid air and heavy rain up from the south. They are not frequent, but they can park over Maui for two or three days, dumping rain and pushing wind from the south rather than the usual northeast trades. When a Kona low is forecast, snorkeling is off the table. Wait it out, enjoy a long breakfast, and hit the reef the day after it clears. The water is usually spectacular the morning after a Kona system passes through.

The practical forecast routine: check the National Weather Service marine forecast for the Maui County zone, look at the wind forecast on Windy or a similar app, and check the swell direction on Surfline or a local surf report. If the wind is under 12 mph and the swell is under two feet from the south or west, you are in for a good snorkel day. Add a look at the actual water from the lanai, and you have all the information you need.

Snorkeling Off the Lawn at Maui Sands Seaside

One of the reasons people come back to unit 711 at O Thank Heaven 4 711 is that the reef starts close. The shoreline below the property has a rocky shelf with patches of sand, and the green sea turtles that graze there are a familiar sight. In November, the turtles are active and easy to spot, especially in the early morning when the water is flat and the sunlight is low and angled.

Entry is the only thing to plan. The lava rock is uneven, so water shoes or sandals help on the walk down and the entry. Look for the sandy gaps near the lawn and walk in slowly rather than stepping onto the reef. Once you are past the first ten yards, the bottom opens up into a mix of coral heads, sand channels, and submerged rock, and the fish life is dense: tangs, butterflyfish, wrasses, parrotfish, and the occasional moray tucked into a crevice. Keep your fins off the coral, keep a respectful distance from the turtles, and you will have one of the best shore dives in West Maui right outside the door.

The lanai is genuinely useful for this. Standing at the railing, you can see the wind direction on the water, watch the pattern of the small white caps, and get a feel for the swell before you commit. That is a small thing, but it is the sort of detail that determines whether you have a great snorkel or a frustrating one. Lining up the reef, the channel, and the island outline of Lanai in the distance is the daily ritual.

Best West Maui Snorkel Spots for November

The reefs along the West Maui coast from Honokowai down to Olowalu are excellent in November, and they are close enough to reach without a long drive. Starting from the north and working south:

Honokowai and Mahinahina. The reef shelf in front of the condo is the first stop, and it is excellent. A few hundred yards north, the little bays around Mahinahina offer similar structure with easier sand entry. Both are quiet and rarely crowded in November.

Kahekili Beach Park (Airport Beach). About ten minutes south, this is one of the most reliable snorkel beaches on the island. There is a parking lot, showers, restrooms, and a wide sandy entry that makes it easy on beginners. The reef runs parallel to the beach in shallow water and is dense with fish. Go early, before the wind picks up, and park near the northern end for the best reef.

Black Rock (Kaanapali). The rocky point at the north end of Kaanapali Beach drops off into deeper water and often holds larger fish and, on a good day, turtles and eagle rays. It is more advanced than Kahekili because of the crowd and the boat traffic. Snorkel close to the rock and keep your head up.

Napili Bay. Ten minutes north of Honokowai, Napili is a postcard beach with a gentle sandy entry and a healthy reef on both sides of the bay. Parking is tight, so plan on arriving by 8 a.m. The water is shallow near shore and gets clearer as you move toward the points.

Olowalu. About twenty minutes south, Olowalu is a long shallow reef famous for coral and turtles. It is best on a calm morning. The entry is rocky in places, so bring water shoes. Many snorkel tour boats stop here for a reason: the coral is excellent.

Further south, the Kihei and Makena coast has plenty of its own options. If you are building a day around the south side, our guide to the best snorkeling spots near Kihei pairs well with this one.

Gear, Timing, and Turtle Etiquette

You do not need much. A quality mask that fits your face, a snorkel with a dry top, and fins are the core kit. A rash guard with a UPF rating, reef-safe sunscreen on exposed skin, and a mesh bag to rinse your gear afterward round it out. If you are flying in and do not want to pack fins, rental shops in Honokowai, Lahaina, and Kaanapali rent full kits for a reasonable daily rate. Our Maui snorkeling gear guide covers fit, brands, and what is worth bringing from home.

Timing is the single biggest factor in a good November snorkel. Get in the water between about 7 and 10 a.m., before the trades build. The water is calmest, the light is best, and the reef is quiet. By noon, the afternoon wind has usually started and the surface gets choppy. Afternoon snorkeling is fine on flat days, but you will see less. If you want to combine snorkeling with other activities, checking the morning marine forecast and then planning the rest of your day around it works well.

The turtle rules are worth knowing. Hawaiian green sea turtles are protected under state and federal law. Stay at least ten feet away, never touch or chase them, and do not block their path to the surface. They need air, and a snorkeler between a turtle and the surface is a real problem for the animal. On the reef, keep your fins off the coral and never stand on it. Coral is slow-growing and easily damaged. The snorkelers who get the most out of a November reef are the ones who move slowly and let the fish come to them.

Early Humpback Season and Other November Wildlife

November is the front edge of humpback whale season in the AuAu Channel, the body of water between Maui, Lanai, and Molokai. The first animals typically appear in late October and early November, mostly adult males and a few pregnant females arriving ahead of the main migration. Sightings are occasional rather than guaranteed, but if you are snorkeling and hear a whale spout or see a tail slap in the distance, that is a good sign of what is to come.

From the lanai at O Thank Heaven 4 711 or from the reef below the property, you can sometimes spot spouts and breaches in November before the population really fills in. The channel view directly west toward Lanai is the ideal angle for this. By December, the whales are everywhere, and from January through March, the channel is crowded with them. If whale watching is a priority, our guide to humpback whale watching in Maui covers the season, the best viewing spots, and what to expect month by month.

The reef itself has its own November wildlife. Green sea turtles are abundant and easy to see off the shoreline. Reef fish are active, including the usual mix of tangs, surgeonfish, and wrasses. If you are patient and stay still near a coral head, you will often see cleaner wrasses at work on larger fish and the occasional octopus tucked into a crevice. Frequently Asked Questions

Is November a good month to snorkel in Maui?

Yes. November is one of the better months for Maui snorkeling. Trade winds tend to lighten as fall transitions toward winter, mornings are often glassy, and water temperatures stay around 77 to 79 F. Visibility commonly runs 40 to 80 feet on calm days, and the first humpback whales of the season usually appear in the AuAu Channel.

What is the water temperature in Maui in November?

Ocean temperatures off West Maui in November hover between 77 and 79 F. Most visitors are comfortable in a swimsuit for 30 to 45 minutes, though a thin 1mm or 2mm rash guard or springsuit helps on longer sessions and on breezy mornings.

Can you snorkel right off the shore at Maui Sands Seaside?

Yes. The shoreline at Maui Sands Seaside in Honokowai has a rocky reef edge that makes for easy entry snorkeling. Green sea turtles graze on the algae there, and fish life is dense along the reef shelf. Enter at the sandy gaps near the lawn and keep fins off the coral.

What time of day is best for snorkeling in November?

Early morning, from about 7 to 10 a.m., is best. Winds are usually lightest before they build in the afternoon, the water is calmest, and sunlight angles into the reef at a low enough angle to reduce glare on the surface. Afternoon snorkeling is still fine on flat days but expect more chop and lower visibility.

Will I see whales while snorkeling in November?

Possibly. November is the front edge of humpback season in the AuAu Channel, so sightings are still occasional rather than guaranteed. If you see a spout or a tail slap from the reef, it is worth pausing and watching. Peak whale activity runs January through March.
