Maui November Packing List: Weather and Wardrobe for 2026

November is one of the most underrated months to be on Maui's west coast. The summer crowds thin out, the trade winds soften, and the AuAu Channel starts to fill with the first humpback whales of the season. What catches people off guard is the range: a sunny 84-degree afternoon on the beach can turn into a breezy 68-degree evening on the lanai within a few hours. This maui november packing list is built around that swing, so you bring layers that actually work and leave behind the stuff that just takes up suitcase space.

What November Weather Really Feels Like on West Maui

Forget the vague "tropical paradise" forecast. Here is what you can actually plan around in the Napili-Honokowai corridor, the stretch of coast just north of Lahaina where Maui Sands Seaside sits. Daytime highs land between 82 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit most days. The ocean holds right around 78 degrees, which is warm enough to stay in for an hour without a wetsuit. Trade winds blow at 10 to 20 mph on a typical afternoon, and that breeze is what makes a west-facing lanai so pleasant at four in the afternoon.

Evenings are the surprise. Once the sun drops behind Lanai, temperatures slide into the upper 60s and low 70s. On a seventh-floor lanai with nothing blocking the wind, that feels cooler than the number suggests, especially after a day of sun. If you are sitting out watching the Molokai silhouettes go dark with a glass of wine, you will want something with sleeves.

November is also the shoulder between Maui's drier summer and its wetter winter. Rain is not a washout -- it usually arrives as a short passing shower, often overnight or early morning -- but it happens more often than in July. The leeward west side gets far less of it than Hana or the windward slopes, which is one reason staying on this coast in November works so well. You can wake up to a shower and be snorkeling off the lawn by nine.

One more thing worth knowing: November sits on the edge of humpback season. The first whales usually show up in the AuAu Channel in late October and early November, with sightings building toward a February peak. Bring binoculars if you have a decent pair. You may catch a distant blow from the lanai, and on a good morning, a full breach.

The Core Wardrobe: Layers Over Volume

The best approach for a November trip is a small capsule of clothing that layers easily. Think in terms of three tiers you can combine in different ways rather than a separate outfit for every day.

Base layer (worn most of the time): four to five short-sleeve tees or tanks, two or three pairs of shorts, one or two swimsuits (bring two so one can dry overnight in humid air), and a lightweight sundress or two if that is your style. Quick-drying fabrics are worth the small upgrade here -- cotton takes forever to dry in Maui humidity, and nothing feels worse than a damp shirt at dinner.

Mid layer: one long-sleeve sun shirt or rash guard for snorkeling and beach days, and one light button-up or linen shirt for dinners. A rash guard does double duty on windy afternoons when you want sun protection without sunscreen.

Top layer: a single thin sweatshirt or cardigan and a packable windbreaker. That is genuinely all you need. You are not packing for cold weather; you are packing for air conditioning in restaurants and a 15-degree drop on the lanai at sunset.

Add two pairs of shoes: comfortable walking sandals or flip-flops for daily wear, and one pair of closed-toe shoes or trail runners if you plan to hike, do the Road to Hana, or drive up Haleakala. Beach, Reef, and Snorkel Gear Worth Packing

The snorkeling off the lawn at Maui Sands Seaside is one of the best reasons to stay on this particular stretch of coast. The reef starts shallow and holds fish, and green sea turtles are regular visitors. Here is what to bring so you can take advantage of it.

Reef-safe sunscreen. Hawaii law bans sunscreens containing oxybenzone and octinoxate, and many properties require reef-safe formulas. Buy it before you travel or plan on paying a premium at a resort shop. Mineral-based zinc or titanium formulas are the safe default. Bring a stick for your face and a larger bottle for the group.

Your own mask and snorkel. Rental gear works, but a mask that fits your face makes an enormous difference in how much you enjoy the water. If you already own a set, pack it -- it weighs almost nothing. Fins are bulkier; many condos provide basic snorkel sets, so check before you haul yours across the Pacific. Our snorkeling gear guide walks through what is worth buying versus borrowing.

Water shoes. Not essential at every entry point on this coast, but useful if you plan to explore rocky shoreline or tide pools. A light pair packs flat.

A dry bag or waterproof phone pouch. Small, cheap, and it will save your phone on a kayak or a dinghy ride. A packable beach bag or mesh tote is also worth thirty seconds of suitcase space.

What not to pack: beach towels. Most oceanfront condos stock them, and they eat suitcase volume. Confirm with your host and skip them if they are provided.

Evening and Dinner Wear for Cooler Lanai Nights

Maui's dinner scene is casual almost everywhere on the west side. A collared shirt and shorts will get you into nearly any restaurant in Lahaina, Kaanapali, or Kapalua. What matters more in November is having something warm enough to sit outside comfortably.

Pack one outfit that reads slightly nicer than your beach clothes -- a sundress, a linen shirt with light trousers, or a simple jumpsuit -- for a nicer dinner. Then add the windbreaker and cardigan you already have. The combination covers everything from a casual plate of fish tacos to a sunset dinner where the temperature dips more than you expected.

Gear for Haleakala, Road to Hana, and Whale Watching

November is a great month for day trips, but two of the most popular ones require gear that beach-focused travelers forget.

Haleakala sunrise. The summit sits at over 10,000 feet and is often near or below freezing before dawn, even in November. You need real layers: a warm fleece or insulated jacket, hat, gloves, and long pants. This is the one item on the list where people genuinely regret under-packing. If you do not own winter gear, pack a warm fleece and a beanie and accept that you will be cold for an hour. It is worth it. See our Haleakala sunrise guide for timing and permit details.

Road to Hana. November brings more rain to the windward side, so a light rain shell and quick-dry clothing matter more than in summer. Bring swimwear for the waterfalls and pools, a towel, and water shoes for the slippery green-rock entries. The drive is long; a small cooler and refillable bottle make it better. Our Road to Hana tips cover the stops worth making.

Whale watching. If you are booking a boat trip in late November, pack a windproof layer and a hat with a strap. It is warmer than a Haleakala summit but much windier than the beach. Binoculars are optional on a boat and helpful from shore. On a calm morning, the seventh-floor lanai at unit 711 gives you a surprisingly good angle on the channel.

What to Put in Your Carry-On

Checked bags get delayed, and Maui's interisland connections do not always wait. Keep your first 24 hours on your back.

Pack one swimsuit, one change of clothes, your reef-safe sunscreen, your mask and snorkel if you own them, any prescription medications, and a small toiletry kit. That is enough to hit the beach the afternoon you land while your suitcase catches up. Add a reusable water bottle -- you will be drinking more than you think in the sun, and Hawaii's tap water is excellent. A foldable sun hat and sunglasses with a strap belong in the same bag.

What to Leave at Home

Maui's grocery stores are good and the local markets are better than most visitors expect. You do not need to bring a week's worth of snacks or a full-size bottle of shampoo. There is a grocery run worth planning, but it happens after you land, not before you leave. Our grocery shopping guide will point you to the right stores near Napili.

Skip the heavy jacket, the bulky beach towels, the hard-sided umbrella that turns inside out in the trades, and the formal outfit you will never wear. Skip the oversize suitcase, too. November travel is easier to pack lightly because you can rely on in-unit laundry if your condo has it. Fill those saved pounds with sunscreen, a good hat, and one warm layer you will be grateful for at sunset.

Pack right, and November on the west coast becomes one of the easiest Maui trips you will ever take.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the weather like in Maui in November?

Expect daytime highs around 82 to 85 degrees Fahrenheit along the West Maui coast, with water temperatures near 78 degrees. Evenings cool to the upper 60s and low 70s. November is the transition into the wetter season, so short passing showers are more common, especially on windward slopes, while the leeward Napili-Honokowai shoreline stays mostly sunny. Trade winds blow most days at 10 to 20 mph.

Do I need long pants and a jacket in Maui in November?

Yes, pack at least one pair of light long pants and one thin layer such as a sweatshirt, cardigan, or packable windbreaker. You will want them for breezy lanai evenings, restaurants with strong air conditioning, and any early morning trip up Haleakala, where summit temperatures can drop into the 40s.

Can I snorkel in Maui in November?

Absolutely. Ocean temperatures hold near 78 degrees through November, and visibility is often excellent before winter swells arrive. Morning is the calmest window. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a rash guard for sun and wind protection, and water shoes if you plan to enter at rocky points. Swell days do happen, so check conditions before getting in.

Is November a good time for whale watching in Maui?

November marks the very start of humpback whale season in the AuAu Channel. Sightings are possible but not guaranteed this early. By late November, the first whales are usually visible from west-facing lanais, and the season builds toward a February peak. Bring binoculars if you want to spot blows from shore.

What should I not bother packing for a November Maui trip?

Skip heavy jackets, bulky towels, full-size toiletries, and hard-sided umbrellas that fight the trade winds. Beach towels and often beach chairs and snorkel gear are provided at many oceanfront condos, so confirm before you pack. Leave room instead for reef-safe sunscreen, a reusable water bottle, and a small day bag for beach hopping.
