Kamaole Beach Parks Kihei Maui: The Complete Guide to Beaches I, II and III

Three crescent beaches, three different personalities, and all of them within a short walk of each other on South Kihei Road. Here is how to pick the right Kamaole beach park for a calm morning swim, a family afternoon, or a sunset snorkel, and what you need to know about parking before you leave the condo.

The Kamaole beach parks are the reason so many visitors end up returning to Kihei instead of trying a different part of Maui each trip. They are county parks, not private resort beaches, which means public access, lifeguards, showers, restrooms and grassy lawns that make a full beach day easy to pull off with kids, coolers and too many towels. The three parks run in a line along South Kihei Road, separated by rocky points that give each beach its own shape and its own set of conditions.

If you are staying on this stretch of the coast, the entire chain is walkable. Our own building sits just up the road at 4711 S Kihei Rd, which puts all three parks within a few minutes on foot -- handy when you want to test the water before committing to a spot for the day. More on that below.

Kamaole Beach Park I: The All-Rounder

Kamaole I is the northernmost and usually the busiest of the three. It has the widest strip of sand, the longest stretch of lawn, and the most complete facilities: restrooms, outdoor showers, picnic tables and a lifeguard tower. The sand slopes gently into the water, and the swimming zone is broad, which makes it the default choice for families with young children and anyone who wants a bit of space.

The southern end of the beach has a rocky point that holds fish, so you will see snorkelers working the edges early in the morning. It is not the best snorkeling in South Maui, but it is convenient and often clear before the trade winds pick up around mid-morning. Parking is a lot off South Kihei Road plus some street parking; it fills on weekends and during school holidays, so arrive before 9 a.m. if you are driving.

Kamaole Beach Park II: Best for Snorkeling and Sunsets

Kamaole II is the middle beach and the one locals tend to favor for a quick swim. It is smaller and steeper than Beach I, which means the sand drops off faster and the water gets deeper sooner -- good for adults who actually want to swim, less ideal for toddlers. The reef structure at both ends of the cove is the most interesting of the three parks, with turtles often grazing on the algae near the rocks.

Because the cove faces west, it also catches the sunset beautifully. Bring a beach chair and stay late. If you want a longer list of evening options nearby, our guide to the best sunset spots in Maui covers the rest of the coast. Parking here is the tightest of the three; there is a small lot plus street parking, and it is worth walking over from your rental rather than circling.

Kamaole Beach Park III: Quieter, With the Best Lawn

Kamaole III, at the southern end, is the largest of the three in terms of park area and the one that feels most like a proper picnic park. The lawn is expansive, there are shaded tables, and the beach itself is broad with a gentler entry than Beach II. It is a strong pick for a long afternoon when you want to alternate between sand, shade and a book.

The reef at the northern tip is worth a look at low tide, and the sandbar that forms off the middle of the beach creates shallow water that kids love. Facilities include restrooms, showers and a lifeguard. The parking lot is larger than Beach II's but still fills on busy days, and there is a small canoe club area at the south end that is not for public use.

Practical Details: Parking, Facilities and Timing

All three parks are free and open to the public, with no entry fee and no permit needed for a normal beach day. Showers are cold-water outdoor types, so plan accordingly. Restrooms are generally open during daylight hours and locked at night.

Timing matters more than most visitors expect. Mornings before about 10 a.m. bring the calmest water and the clearest conditions for snorkeling. Trade winds typically build through late morning and afternoon, chopping up the surface and reducing visibility. Afternoon is better for lounging and bodyboarding than for looking underwater. Winter surf can occasionally make all three beaches rough, and the lifeguards will post warnings when that happens.

Where the Kamaole Parks Fit in a Kihei Stay

One of the underrated pleasures of staying on this part of South Kihei Road is not having to drive to the beach at all. From the building at 4711 S Kihei Rd, Beach I is a short walk north, Beach II a few minutes further, and Beach III a slightly longer stroll south. That means you can do a sunrise swim, come back for breakfast on the lanai, and head out again in the afternoon without touching the car.

The lanai itself is worth mentioning here because it changes how you use the coast. A west-facing 35-foot deck looking toward Lanai and Molokai is a good place to watch the water conditions before you commit to a beach. In whale season, roughly November through May, humpbacks are often visible in the AuAu Channel from the railing -- and green sea turtles turn up along the shoreline below. A Simple Plan for a Kamaole Beach Day

Start early at Beach II for a snorkel before the wind arrives, then walk north to Beach I for a swim and a shower. Spend the hottest part of the day on the lawn at Beach III under a tree, and finish with sunset back at Beach II or from your own lanai. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, cash for the occasional food truck, and a light jacket for the evening. That is about as good as a Maui beach day gets, and it requires almost no driving.
