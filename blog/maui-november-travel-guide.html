Maui in November Travel Guide: What to Expect

November is one of the most overlooked months on the Maui calendar. The summer crowds have thinned, the trade winds have softened, the water is still warm, and the first humpback whales of the season begin to appear in the AuAu Channel. It is also a month people ask about constantly, usually with some version of the same question: is it a good time, or am I taking a risk with the weather? This Maui in November travel guide lays out what the month actually looks like on the ground, from daily temperatures and ocean conditions to crowds, costs, and the practical logistics of a West Maui stay.

Weather in November: What the Days Actually Feel Like

Start with the numbers, because they explain a lot. On the West Maui coast, daytime highs in November typically sit between 82 and 85 degrees Fahrenheit. Overnight lows drop into the high 60s, which is comfortable enough that most people sleep with the windows open and the ceiling fans on. Ocean temperatures stay near 78 degrees, essentially unchanged from October, so swimming and snorkeling feel warm well into the month.

The honest part of the November story is rain. This is one of the wetter months of the year, and if you look at monthly averages you will see a figure noticeably higher than April or June. What that average hides is the shape of the rain. November showers arrive as short passing cells, often at night or early morning, and they tend to clear within twenty or thirty minutes. An all-day washout is rare. A morning where you wait out a squall on the lanai with coffee and then walk down to the water at ten is extremely common.

Where you stay matters for this. The leeward side of West Maui, running from Lahaina north through Napili and Honokowai, is dramatically drier than the windward side around Hana or the lush upcountry slopes. If a storm system sits over the island for a couple of days, the West side often gets a fraction of the rainfall you would see in Kihei's interior or up towards Kula. In practical terms, a November trip based in Napili-Honokowai gives you the best odds of dry, sunny afternoons.

Trade winds also ease slightly in November compared with the summer months, which means mornings can feel still and warm rather than brisk. The upside is calmer afternoons on west-facing beaches and a more comfortable deck temperature on a lanai that faces the water. The trade-off is that the vog from the Big Island sometimes drifts over, muting the horizon for a day or two before the trades clear it out.

Whale Season Begins: What to Expect in the AuAu Channel

November is technically the opening of humpback whale season in Hawaii, though the phrasing deserves a caveat. The first animals usually appear in the AuAu Channel in late October or the first week of November, and sightings are sparse and genuinely special early on. By the middle of the month, you can often spot a handful of whales on any given morning from a high vantage point. By Thanksgiving, the channel is filling in steadily and blows are visible throughout the day.

This timing makes November a quietly exciting month. You are not yet in the peak of February and March, when dozens of whales are visible at once and the channel feels crowded with life. But you are there for the arrival, and there is something appealing about seeing the first whales of the season rather than the last.

For practical whale watching, the highest-percentage views come from elevated west-facing positions with a wide sweep of the channel. The AuAu Channel runs between Maui, Lanai, and Molokai, so anywhere with a clear line of sight to those islands gives you a chance. Charter boats start running whale-focused trips in November, though the early-season trips are more of a gamble than a mid-winter booking. If you want to maximize your odds without booking anything, spend your mornings on a west-facing lanai with a pair of binoculars.

Ocean Conditions and Snorkeling from the Shore

November water is warm, clear, and generally calm on the leeward coast, with a few caveats. The first north swells of the season begin to arrive in late October and November, and while they mostly affect north-facing beaches like Honolua Bay and the north end of Napili, they can occasionally wrap around and add surge to west-facing reefs for a day or two. On those days, the water gets cloudy and snorkeling is better postponed until things settle.

On a normal November morning, though, the reef off the shoreline at Maui Sands Seaside and the surrounding Napili-Honokowai stretch is excellent. The water is warm enough to spend a long time in without a wetsuit, and the reef holds the usual cast of tangs, butterflyfish, parrotfish, and the occasional moray eel. Hawaiian green sea turtles are common along this shoreline year-round, and November is as good a month as any to see them grazing on the algae patches near the rocks.

If you want a broader survey of snorkeling options, our guide to the best snorkeling spots near Kihei covers the south-side reefs in detail, and the snorkeling gear guide is worth a read before you pack. For the West side specifically, mornings are consistently calmer than afternoons in November, so plan water time before lunch if you can.

Visibility in November typically runs 40 to 80 feet on calm days, occasionally better. That is not quite the crystalline clarity of the driest summer weeks, but it is more than enough for reef snorkeling and the occasional turtle encounter.

Crowds, Costs, and the Thanksgiving Effect

November is a shoulder month, and that is the single biggest reason to consider it. Early-to-mid November is one of the quietest stretches on the Maui calendar. Restaurants have walk-in tables, parking at popular beaches is manageable, and the Road to Hana does not feel like a convoy. If your idea of a good vacation involves not standing in line for a plate lunch, this is your window.

The exception lands in the last week of the month. Thanksgiving week is one of the busiest short windows of the year on Maui, because it combines a mainland holiday with a natural four-day trip. Airfare climbs sharply, rental cars get tight, and vacation rentals fill up months in advance. If your trip overlaps Thanksgiving, book flights and lodging early and expect the roads and beaches to feel closer to peak season for a few days.

Costs follow the same curve. November rates for flights and rentals are typically well below December and January, and often below summer. The one caveat is that some West Maui inventory is still recovering from the 2023 fires, so availability in Lahaina-adjacent areas can be tighter than the raw numbers suggest. Book well ahead if you want a specific property, especially for oceanfront units with a west-facing deck.

Why West Maui Works Especially Well in November

The leeward West Maui coast, from Lahaina north through Honokowai, Kahana, and Napili, is the optimal base for a November trip for three reasons. First, it is drier than the windward side, so the month's rain risk is lower. Second, it faces west across the AuAu Channel, which means the best whale-watching angle on the island once the season gets going. Third, the reef structure along this shoreline supports reliable shore snorkeling and easy turtle encounters.

The trade-off is that West Maui is farther from the south-side beaches around Kihei and Wailea, and farther from Haleakala and the upcountry. If your November trip is focused on the Road to Hana, the summit sunrise, and the south-side beaches, a Kihei base makes more sense. Our comparison of Kihei vs Lahaina walks through that decision in detail. But if your priorities are whales, sunsets over Lanai and Molokai, and warm water snorkeling close to home, the West side is hard to beat.

November is also a good month for sunsets. The sun drops a little further south in the sky than in summer, and the cloud patterns from passing showers produce some genuinely spectacular evening light. On clear nights, Molokai's outline is sharp against the horizon and Lanai's silhouette sits low and dark to the left.

Packing and Practical Details for November

Packing for Maui in November is mostly a warm-weather exercise with a few specific additions. Bring:

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (Hawaii law prohibits oxybenzone and octinoxate formulas, and local shops stock compliant alternatives)
  • A light rain shell or packable jacket for passing showers
  • A long-sleeve layer for breezy evenings and the cool summit of Haleakala if you plan a sunrise trip
  • Closed-toe shoes for hikes and the Road to Hana
  • Snorkel gear if you are particular about fit, or a plan to rent locally
  • A small dry bag for boat days
  • Binoculars for whale watching from the lanai

Two practical notes. First, the whales do not respect a schedule, so give yourself a couple of mornings on the lanai rather than planning an excursion for every day. Second, November is a good month for a relaxed itinerary. The weather rewards flexibility more than any other month of the year, and the rewards are better when you leave room for them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is November a good time to visit Maui?

Yes. November sits in the sweet spot between the summer crowd and the December holiday peak. You get warm weather, manageable prices, and the start of humpback whale season in the AuAu Channel. The main caveat is a higher chance of passing rain showers compared with spring.

What is the weather like in Maui in November?

Expect daytime highs around 82 to 85 degrees Fahrenheit on the West Maui coast and overnight lows in the high 60s. Ocean temperatures hold near 78 degrees Fahrenheit. November is one of the wetter months, but rain typically arrives as short passing showers rather than all-day storms.

Can you see whales in Maui in November?

Yes, but early in the month sightings are sparse. The first humpbacks usually appear in the AuAu Channel in late October or early November, and numbers build steadily through the month. By Thanksgiving you can often spot blows and tail slaps from a west-facing lanai without leaving the property.

Is November a busy season in Maui?

Generally no. November is a shoulder month, and early-to-mid November is one of the quieter stretches of the year. The exception is the week of Thanksgiving, when airfare and rentals spike for a few days before settling back down. Book flights early if your trip overlaps the holiday.

What should I pack for Maui in November?

Pack warm-weather clothing, reef-safe sunscreen, a light rain shell for passing showers, and a long-sleeve layer for breezy evenings and the cool summit of Haleakala. Bring your own snorkel gear or plan to rent locally, and include a small dry bag if you plan to spend time in the water.
