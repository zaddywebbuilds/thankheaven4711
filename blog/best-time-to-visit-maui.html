Best Time to Visit Maui: Month-by-Month Guide

Maui is good year-round. The question is what you want from the trip. If you want humpback whales, January and February are exceptional. If you want lower prices and smaller crowds, September and October are hard to beat. Here is the honest month-by-month breakdown.

Maui's Two Seasons

Maui technically has a dry season (kau) from May through September and a wet season (hooilo) from October through April. On the west and south shores -- where Kihei and Maui Sands Seaside sit -- the distinction is less dramatic than you might expect. The leeward (rain shadow) coast stays dry most of the year. Most rain falls on the windward (northeast) side, which is why Hana is lush and Kihei is golden and brown.

For visitors staying at unit 711, the west-facing, leeward position means reliable sunshine even in the technical wet season. When it does rain in Kihei, it is usually a brief shower, not a multiday event.

Month-by-Month Breakdown

January and February -- Peak whale season, peak prices

The best months to see humpback whales. Thousands of whales fill the Maui Nui basin and breach, blow, and sing within view of shore. The west side is calm and sunny. Prices are at their annual high -- this is high season for Hawaii tourism broadly, partly due to the whales and partly due to mainland winter escapes. Book well in advance.

March and April -- Shoulder season transitioning out of whale season

Whale activity decreases through March as adults begin migrating back to Alaska, though mothers with new calves often stay through April. Crowds thin slightly from the January and February peaks. Ocean conditions are excellent. Spring break (mid-March) is a brief crowd surge. Otherwise, one of the better value windows for the experience you get.

May and June -- Good weather, building summer crowds

Dry season begins. Trade winds are consistent, keeping the leeward coast sunny and comfortable. Memorial Day and the beginning of school holidays in June push visitor numbers up. Prices are moderate. No whale activity. The snorkeling is excellent.

July and August -- Peak summer, second-highest prices

Summer is the second peak season. Beaches and popular attractions are at maximum capacity. Water temperatures are at their warmest (80-82 degrees Fahrenheit). Maui Swap Meet and local activities are in full swing. Budget travelers generally avoid this window. Families with school-age children have little choice.

Any month is worth it

Unit 711 faces west. The west shore is sunny and calm year-round. The question is which bonus you want with your sunshine -- whales in winter, quiet in fall, or warmth in summer.

September and October -- Best value month, quietest period

September and October are the off-peak sweet spot. Crowds are down 30 to 40 percent from summer peaks. Flights and accommodation are at their lowest prices of the year. Weather remains dry and excellent on the west and south shores. The ocean is warm. Whale season has not started yet, but if it matters to you, the very first arrivals sometimes appear in late October or November.

For visitors who want a quality Maui experience without the high-season premium, September and October are arguably the best months on the calendar.

November and December -- Transition into high season

The first humpbacks begin arriving in November. By Thanksgiving, whale sightings are occasional. Through December they become regular. Prices start climbing from the October low and reach peak by Christmas and New Year, which is the most expensive week of the year. The north shore can see winter swell, but the west side remains protected.

Quick Summary by Priority

  • Whale watching: January and February
  • Best value: September and October
  • Families (school schedule): July and August, or spring break March
  • Snorkeling conditions: May through October (calmer, clearest)
  • Fewest crowds: September and October
  • Warmest water: August and September (80-82 degrees F)
