How Much Does a Maui Vacation Cost?

Maui is expensive. That is the honest answer, and the planning should start from there. The island is remote, everything has to be shipped in, and the labor market reflects the cost of living in one of the most sought-after places in the US. A realistic budget prevents the trip from being overshadowed by financial stress on arrival.

Flights

Round-trip flights from the West Coast (Los Angeles, San Francisco, Seattle) to Kahului (OGG) typically run $400 to $700 per person in economy when booked 4 to 8 weeks out. Peak season (December, January, February, June, July) and last-minute bookings push prices significantly higher. From the East Coast, add a connection and $200 to $400 to those figures. Hawaiian Airlines, Alaska Airlines, Southwest, and Delta all serve Maui directly from multiple West Coast cities. Book early, set price alerts, and be flexible on travel days (mid-week is usually cheaper than weekend departures).

Accommodation

This is where the widest range exists. The options, roughly:

  • Budget condos and vacation rentals (Kihei): $150 to $250/night. Usually older units with basic amenities, may not be oceanfront.
  • Quality oceanfront condos (Kihei, South Maui): $250 to $500/night. This is the range where direct bookings like unit 711 sit -- genuine ocean access, full kitchens, laundry, without resort fee structure.
  • Wailea resort hotels: $500 to $1,000+/night before resort fees ($40 to $65/night) and parking ($35 to $50/night).
  • Top-tier resorts (Four Seasons, Montage): $1,200 to $2,500+/night.

For a couple spending 8 nights, the difference between a quality condo direct booking and a mid-range Wailea hotel can easily be $2,000 to $3,500. The condo vs hotel math runs strongly in favor of condos for stays of five or more nights.

Car Rental

A car is necessary. Maui has no meaningful public transit. Rental cars run $60 to $120 per day depending on season and lead time. Book early -- Maui has had recurring car rental shortages during peak periods. Compact or economy vehicles are fine for most of the island, though a mid-size SUV is worth considering for the Road to Hana and Haleakala roads.

Food

Food costs depend entirely on how much you cook vs. eat out. The realistic ranges:

  • Restaurant breakfast for two: $30 to $70 (resort hotel) or $20 to $35 (Kihei local spots)
  • Plate lunch or poke bowl: $12 to $20 per person
  • Mid-range dinner for two: $60 to $120 with drinks
  • Nicer dinner (Monkeypod, Five Palms): $120 to $200 for two
  • Mama's Fish House: $200 to $300+ for two

A condo kitchen cuts food costs dramatically. Buying groceries from Costco or Foodland and cooking breakfast and lunch saves $60 to $100 per day. A week-long couple's trip with a kitchen and smart grocery shopping can bring food costs down to $600 to $900 for the week, versus $1,400 to $2,000 eating out for every meal.

Activities

Most of what makes Maui special is free -- beaches, reef snorkeling, sunsets, scenery. The paid experiences worth budgeting for:

  • Molokini snorkel tour: $80 to $120 per person
  • Whale watch tour (in season): $40 to $80 per person
  • Sunset sail: $90 to $140 per person
  • Surfing lesson: $60 to $90 per person
  • Haleakala timed-entry: $30 per vehicle ($25 + reservation fee)
  • Maui Ocean Center: $35 to $45 per adult

For a couple doing a reasonable selection of activities over eight nights, budget $400 to $600 for activities total.

Sample Budgets (8 Nights, 2 Adults)

  • Budget-conscious: ~$4,500 to $6,000 total (West Coast flights, quality condo, kitchen meals most days, selective activities)
  • Mid-range: ~$7,000 to $10,000 total (flights, quality oceanfront condo, mix of cooking and dining out, key activities)
  • Resort experience: ~$12,000 to $20,000+ total (flights, Wailea resort, daily dining out, full activity budget)
